Diagrams

In a group, on paper or in diagrams.net: draw four classes, compare them, then pull what they share into a parent class.

What you are building

A game needs ways for its characters to get around. Your group designs a class for each of four modes of transportation: Moped, Car, Semi-Truck and Hovercraft. Then you look for what they have in common and draw a parent class that holds it, with child classes that keep only what makes each one different.

No project, no code, nothing to run. Notepad, pencil and paper, diagrams.net or lucidchart.com all work. The next exercise turns your drawing into Java.

Where people get stuck

  • Step 1 already shares things. Draft each class as if the others did not exist. The overlap is what Step 2 is for.
  • A parent with everything in it. The parent holds only what ALL of your items have in common. Something only some share can go in a middle class: more than one level is allowed.
  • Copying the parent's items into every child. A child lists only what makes it different. If it appears in the parent, it is already inherited.
  • No idea what a car or a hovercraft needs. Think of your character: how does it get in, move and stop? Look it up if you want.

The one idea

Find what repeats, write it once. A parent class is the part your classes share; each child is only what is different.
