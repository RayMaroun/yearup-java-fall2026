The Capstone 2 Path

The whole capstone on one page, in the order to do it. Each step links to the page that helps with it. Tick a step when it is done, and you always know where you are.

Read the text firstThese pages help you understand and build. They do not replace your capstone text: the text is what your project is checked against.
Design before codeThis capstone starts with a class diagram. You write no code until Raymond gave you the green light for it.
Two short checksYou do not have to ask for them. Raymond calls everyone, in alphabetical order, twice.
On your own next weekIn the build week you work alone. Everything you need is gathered on the Week 8 page.

Steps0 of 10

    1

    Read your capstone text, all of it

    before anything elseReading Your Capstone Text

    Do

    1. Read your text from the first page to the last, once, without stopping to design.
    2. Then open Reading Your Capstone Text: it shows where each rule is and what the words mean.

    Done looks like

    You can say, without looking, what the shop sells, what costs extra, and what the application does from the first screen to the receipt.

    Watch out

    • Do not start from the screens page alone. The rules are on the pages before it.
    2

    Choose your option

    Tuesday, by 11:30 am Eastern

    Do

    1. Deli, Pizza, Taco or Custom: the four have the same rules and the same screens. Only the food changes.
    2. Custom is the same capstone with a shop you invent. Explain your idea to Raymond first: you need his yes before you go on.
    3. Tell Raymond which option you take.

    Done looks like

    Raymond knows your option, and for Custom he approved your idea.

    Watch out

    • Pick the one you find easiest to picture. No option is worth more than another.
    3

    Find your classes

    part 1 of the class diagram guideFrom the Text to Your Classes

    Do

    1. Work through From the Text to Your Classes: read with two colors, sort your words, make the seven decisions.
    2. Write your classes down as a list: for each one, what it holds and what it can do.

    Done looks like

    A list of classes on paper or in a file, and a sentence for each of the seven decisions.

    Watch out

    • No drawing yet. A drawing made before the decisions gets redrawn.
    4

    Check 1 with Raymond

    Tuesday, from 2:00 pm Eastern, alphabetical order

    Do

    1. Be ready at 2:00 pm Eastern with your list and your seven decisions.
    2. Raymond calls you when it is your turn. It takes a few minutes.
    3. You leave with what to change, if anything.

    Done looks like

    You talked to Raymond once, and you know your design is going in a good direction.

    Watch out

    • Not finished at 2:00? Show what you have. That is the point of this check.
    • While you wait for your turn, keep working: set up your repository, your project board and your README.
    5

    Draw your diagram

    part 2Drawing It in diagrams.netClass Diagram Notation

    Do

    1. Work through Drawing It in diagrams.net: one box, the lines, the five passes, the four drawing mistakes.
    2. Save the diagram file in your project.

    Done looks like

    Every class is a box with three bands, and every line is attached at both ends.

    Watch out

    • Use diagrams.net. The capstone asks for the diagram file itself, with a picture of it.
    6

    Check your own diagram, and push it

    part 3 · pushed by Wednesday 12:00 noon EasternCheck Your Diagram

    Do

    1. Work through Check Your Diagram: walk one order through it, look for the six traps.
    2. Commit and push the .drawio file and its picture by Wednesday 12:00 noon Eastern, finished or not.

    Done looks like

    Your diagram is in your repository on GitHub, and you can explain every box.

    Watch out

    • The noon push is for everyone. An unfinished diagram on time can still be fixed in the afternoon.
    7

    Check 2 with Raymond: the green light

    Wednesday, from 1:00 pm Eastern, alphabetical order

    Do

    1. Be ready at 1:00 pm Eastern with your diagram open.
    2. Raymond calls you. He asks you to explain your boxes and walks an order through them with you.
    3. If something has to change: change it, push, and tell him. He looks again the same afternoon.

    Done looks like

    Raymond gave you the green light. Everyone has it by Wednesday 4:00 pm Eastern.

    Watch out

    • No green light, no code. Thursday and Friday are holidays: the diagram has to be settled on Wednesday.
    8

    Set up your project

    your own job this time

    Do

    1. A public GitHub repository with a meaningful name.
    2. A GitHub project board with your user stories, made from the requirements.
    3. A README from the course template. It will show the picture of your class diagram.

    Done looks like

    The repository, the board and the README exist before Monday, November 30.

    Watch out

    • There is no class time for the setup in this capstone. The waiting time on Tuesday and Wednesday is a good moment.
    9

    Build

    Monday, November 30 to Thursday, December 3

    Do

    1. Build from your diagram, one small piece at a time, and commit after each piece.
    2. When the code changes a class, change the diagram the same day.
    3. The pages for the build week are on the Week 8 page, under Build.

    Done looks like

    Every screen and every rule of your text works, and your diagram still matches your code.

    Watch out

    • Build the classes and their prices first, the screens after. A screen is easy once the classes answer correctly.
    10

    Demo Day

    Friday, December 4

    Do

    1. Your README shows what the project does, how to run it, and the picture of your class diagram.
    2. You submit your repository link in Brightspace.
    3. You present your application and explain one interesting piece of your code.

    Done looks like

    Submitted in Brightspace, presented, and you could explain any line you were asked about.

    Watch out

    • You are asked about your own code. Code you cannot explain counts against you.

    If you only remember one thing

    Text, then design, then code. Read the whole text, decide your classes, draw them, get the green light, and only then build. Every hour spent on the design saves several in the build week.
