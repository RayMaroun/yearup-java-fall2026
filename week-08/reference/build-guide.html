Build Guide

Your diagram has the green light and the project is empty: here is what to do next, in order. Fourteen steps for the build week, Monday November 30 to Thursday December 3. Each step says what to build, how you check it, what usually goes wrong, and what to commit. Tick a step when its "done looks like" is true on your screen.

Classes first, screens afterSteps 2 to 6 have no screens at all. You build your classes and check every price from main. A screen is easy once the classes answer correctly. Learners who start with the screens lose days.
Commit after every stepAt least one commit per step, more is better. Each message is type: what changed (feat, fix, docs, refactor, chore), a blank line, then bullets on what you did. The shape is on The Commit Message.
Stuck for 20 minutes?Say which step and what you expected to see. "Step 4, I expected 11.50 and it prints 10.50" gets you an answer fast.
Your text decidesThese pages help you build. Your capstone text is what your project is checked against. When this page and your text seem to disagree, your text wins: tell Raymond.

    The classes and their prices · no screens yet

    2

    Your classes as empty shells

    Class Diagram GuideMon Nov 30

    Do

    1. Open your diagram next to IntelliJ. Create every box of it in src/main/java/com/pluralsight, with the same name, the same kind (whatever your diagram says it is) and the same lines between boxes.
    2. Give each one its fields and its constructors, exactly as in the diagram. No behavior yet: no prices worked out, no screens, no file.
    3. Every price is money, so every price is a BigDecimal, built from the text of the price (like "7.00"), never from a decimal number.
    4. Compile (Build, Build Project).

    Done looks like

    The project compiles with no red. You can put your finger on any box of the diagram and open the matching file in IntelliJ, and the other way round.

    Watch out

    • Something you need is not in the diagram? Add it to the code AND to the diagram the same day. The diagram has to match the code at the end.
    • Do not start a menu "just to see something". The screens are step 7.

    Commit

    feat: add the classes from the class diagram - fields and constructors only, no behavior yet
    3

    Your toppings and their prices

    How a Price Adds UpMon Nov 30

    Do

    1. On paper first: from the price table in your text, write what one topping of each kind should cost for each of the three sizes. One from the first premium group, one from the second, one regular topping, one sauce. Then the two premium ones again with an extra portion.
    2. Now give your design what it needs to work those prices out. However your design does it, the rules are the text's: a premium topping costs its price for the size; an extra portion ADDS the extra price to that; regular toppings and sauces are included and have no extra portion.
    3. In main, make one of each kind and print its price for each of the three sizes, with two decimals.

    Done looks like

    Every number printed equals the number on your paper. With the Deli's table, for example:

    4" 8" 12" steak 1.00 2.00 3.00 steak, extra 1.50 3.00 4.50 american 0.75 1.50 2.25 american, extra 1.05 2.10 3.15 lettuce, mayo included, nothing added

    Those are the Deli's numbers. Use the table in your own text.

    Watch out

    • Steak with an extra portion on an 8" is 3.00, not 1.00: the extra is added to the normal price, it does not replace it.
    • The prices change with the size. A topping that costs the same in all three sizes is a bug.
    • A regular topping or a sauce that adds money, or that can get an extra portion, goes against the text.
    • 3.0 or 3.000 printed instead of 3.00: money is shown with two decimals.

    Commit

    feat: add topping prices - premium toppings priced by size - extra portion adds the extra price - checked all three sizes from main
    4

    One main item and its price

    How a Price Adds UpBuilding One ItemMon Nov 30

    Do

    1. On paper: choose one item (a type, a size, a few toppings, one premium with an extra portion, the special option on) and add up its price from the table.
    2. In main, build that same item in code, not through a screen, and print its price.
    3. Print the item too, with everything on it: type, size, every topping, which ones are extra, the special option. You will need that text at checkout and in the receipt.
    4. Try a second one: another size, no toppings at all.

    Done looks like

    The price printed equals your sum on paper. The Deli's example: an 8" sandwich on wheat with steak, an extra portion of steak, american cheese, lettuce, mayo, toasted:

    8" sandwich 7.00 steak 2.00 extra steak 1.00 american cheese 1.50 lettuce, mayo included toasted no extra cost total 11.50

    The same item (middle size, one premium with an extra portion, one from the second group) is 16.50 for the Pizza (Medium 12"), 13.50 for the Taco (3-Taco Plate) and 12.00 for Custom (Size 2). A Deli 4" with nothing on it is 5.50.

    Watch out

    • The type (bread, crust, shell) does not change the price. The size does.
    • The special option is free. If toasting adds money, check where.
    • Taco: a 3-Taco Plate is one item, priced once, not three times.
    • An item with no premium toppings is allowed, and so is one with the same premium twice.

    Commit

    feat: add the price of the main item - size price plus every premium topping - checked 11.50 by hand

    The drink and the third product

    What an Order Can HoldMon Nov 30

    Do

    1. In main, make a drink in each of the three sizes and print each one with its price.
    2. Make your third product (chips, garlic knots, chips & salsa, or your snack) and print it with its price.

    Done looks like

    Drinks: Small 2.00, Medium 2.50, Large 3.00, the flavor printed with it. The third product: 1.50, with its type when your text asks for one (chips, chips & salsa, snack). Same numbers in all four options.

    Watch out

    • The third product has one size and one price. The flavor of a drink does not change its price.
    • Pizza: garlic knots have no type to choose.

    Commit

    feat: add drinks and chips - drink priced by size - chips at one price
    6

    The order and its total

    What an Order Can HoldMon Nov 30

    Do

    1. In main, put several products on one order: the item from step 4, a drink, a third product. Print the whole order and its total.
    2. Then make an order with nothing on it. Print its total, and print whether your program can tell it is empty. Checkout will need that.
    3. Then an order with only a drink on it.

    Done looks like

    The total equals your sum on paper. The Deli: the 11.50 sandwich, a Medium drink 2.50 and chips 1.50 give 15.50. (Pizza 20.50, Taco 17.50, Custom 16.00 with the same choices.) The empty order totals 0.00 and your program knows it is empty. The drink-only order totals 2.00, 2.50 or 3.00 and is not empty.

    Watch out

    • A total that is off by a few cents means a price was not built from text somewhere.
    • An order with zero main items is allowed, as long as it has a drink or the third product. Only a completely empty order cannot be checked out.
    • Two items of the same kind on one order must both be there and both count.

    Commit

    feat: add the order total - order holds items, drinks and chips - checked 15.50 by hand, empty order is 0.00

    Only now, the screens

    7

    Home screen

    Menu Flow MapTue Dec 1

    Do

    1. Move your checks from steps 3 to 6 out of main, to a separate class you can run on its own or to a note, so you can run them again later. Now main starts the application.
    2. The Home screen shows 1) New Order and 0) Exit. It comes back again and again until the user chooses Exit.
    3. Anything else typed: a message, and the question again.
    4. For now, New Order can print "order screen" and come back. Step 8 fills it in.

    Done looks like

    Run it and type 7, abc, and just Enter: each time a message, then the Home screen again, no crash. 0 ends the program.

    Watch out

    • Letters typed where a number is expected must not crash the program. Read every answer as a whole line.
    • One Scanner on the keyboard for the whole program. A second one makes questions skip.

    Commit

    feat: add home screen - loops until Exit - asks again on wrong input
    8

    Order screen

    Menu Flow MapWhat an Order Can HoldTue Dec 1

    Do

    1. New Order starts a new, empty order and shows the Order screen.
    2. The Order screen shows the items added so far, then the five options of your text with their numbers: 1) Add (your item), 2) Add Drink, 3) Add (your third product), 4) Checkout and 0) Cancel Order, in that order.
    3. Cancel Order deletes the order and goes back to the Home screen. The other four can print a placeholder for now.
    4. To see the list working before step 9 exists, you may add the step 4 item by hand for a test run, then take it out.

    Done looks like

    Home, 1, the Order screen with nothing on it. 0, back Home. 1 again: a new order, empty. Wrong input on the Order screen: a message and the screen again.

    Watch out

    • After Cancel Order, the next New Order must start empty. If the old items are still there, the order was not deleted.
    • Same menu numbers as the text, in the same order.

    Commit

    feat: add order screen - shows the items so far and the five options - Cancel Order deletes the order and goes home
    9

    The Add item screen, one question at a time

    Building One ItemMenu Flow MapTue Dec 1 to Wed Dec 2

    Do

    1. The questions, in the order of your text: the type, the size, the first premium group (any number, and after each one "Extra?"), the second premium group (the same), the regular toppings (any number), the sauces (any number), the special option (yes or no).
    2. Write ONE question, run it, check it, then the next. After each one, print the item so far and its price.
    3. Show the choices with numbers, and make it clear how to say "no more" for the questions that take any number.
    4. At the end, the item goes on the order and the Order screen comes back. One item per Add.

    Done looks like

    Build the step 4 item through the screens: the Order screen lists it with all its toppings, and its price is the same as in step 4 (11.50 for the Deli example). Then build one with no premium toppings and no regular toppings: that works too.

    Watch out

    • Type first, then size. The text gives the order of the questions.
    • "Extra?" is asked only after each premium topping, never after a regular topping or a sauce.
    • "None" must be possible for every "any number" question.
    • A wrong answer to any question shows a message and asks THAT question again. It does not start the item over and does not crash.

    Commit

    One commit per question or two, for example:

    feat: ask for the type and the size feat: ask for premium toppings with Extra? feat: ask for regular toppings and sauces feat: ask for the special option

    2. The third product, as your text says: the Deli asks the chip type, the Taco the salsa type, Custom the snack type; the Pizza's garlic knots are added with no question. Then back to the Order screen.

    11

    Checkout

    Menu Flow MapWhat an Order Can HoldWed Dec 2

    Do

    1. Checkout shows the order details: every item with all its toppings (and which are extra, and the special option), every drink, every third product, then the total price.
    2. An empty order cannot be checked out: a message, and the customer stays on the Order screen to add something.
    3. Then 1) Confirm and 0) Cancel. Confirm creates the receipt file (step 12; for now it can print "receipt") and goes back to the Home screen. Cancel deletes the order and goes back to the Home screen.

    Done looks like

    The step 6 order checked out shows every product and a total of 15.50 (the Deli example). Checkout on a new, empty order is refused. After Confirm or Cancel, the next New Order starts empty.

    Watch out

    • The total on screen must equal the sum of the lines above it. Add them up by hand once.
    • A customer must be able to check every topping on the checkout screen. "1 sandwich 11.50" is not enough.
    • Cancel here deletes the order, it does not go back to the Order screen.

    Commit

    feat: add checkout - shows order details and total - refuses an empty order - Confirm and Cancel go back home
    12

    The receipt file

    The Receipt FileDate PatternsWed Dec 2 to Thu Dec 3

    Do

    1. When the customer confirms, write the order to a new file in a folder receipts inside your project folder.
    2. The file name is the date and time the order was confirmed, with the pattern yyyyMMdd-HHmmss and .txt, for example 20230329-121523.txt.
    3. The content is the same order details shown at checkout, with the total.

    Done looks like

    Confirm two orders a few seconds apart. In IntelliJ's Project panel, the receipts folder (right-click, Reload from Disk) shows two files, named from the time you confirmed. Open one: it reads like the checkout screen, same lines, same total.

    Watch out

    • If the receipts folder does not exist yet, the first save fails. Make sure it exists before writing.
    • HH is the 24-hour clock, hh is not. MM is the month, mm the minutes.
    • One file per order. If the second order replaced the first, the name did not change.
    • File work can fail: handle it, and close what you open.

    Commit

    feat: save the receipt file - one file per confirmed order in receipts - name from date and time, yyyyMMdd-HHmmss

    Test like a customer

    Menu Flow MapThu Dec 3

    Do

    Run the application and place these orders by hand. For each, write the total you expect from the price table BEFORE you run it.

    1. A full order: two main items (one with extra portions, one plain), two drinks, a third product. Total checked by hand, Confirm, receipt opened.
    2. A drink only. It can be checked out.
    3. An empty order at Checkout. It is refused.
    4. Wrong input everywhere: a letter, a number not on the menu, an empty Enter, on every screen and at every question.
    5. Cancel Order on the Order screen, and Cancel at Checkout. Both go Home, both delete the order, no receipt.
    6. Three orders in a row without restarting, then Exit.

    Done looks like

    Every total equals your hand sum. Nothing crashed. One receipt per confirmed order, none for the cancelled ones.

    Watch out

    • Fix each bug as you find it, one commit per fix, and run the whole list again at the end.
    • Read your text's screens page once more, line by line, and tick each line on the running program.

    Commit

    fix: ask the size again on wrong input

    If you only remember one thing

    Every number your program prints, you worked out by hand first. Build the classes and check their prices from main on Monday, then the screens, one question at a time, then the file. When a total is wrong, you know which step to go back to.
